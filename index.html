THE BEAUTY OF THE PAST

Letters Tell Stories.

Retro typography is more than just lettering. It represents different eras, cultures, and artistic movements. From elegant Art Deco lettering to bold psychedelic designs, typography has always reflected the personality of its time.

A WALK THROUGH TIME

The History of Retro Typography

1920s

Art Deco Elegance

GEOMETRIC. ELEGANT. BOLD.

The 1920s introduced Art Deco typography. Inspired by modern architecture and luxury, lettering featured geometric shapes, symmetrical designs, and elegant lines.

1950s

Mid-Century Modern

PLAYFUL. FRIENDLY. EXPRESSIVE.

Typography became more playful and welcoming. Rounded letters, handwritten scripts, and colorful advertising became popular in restaurants, advertisements, and packaging.

1960s–70s

Psychedelic Typography

COLORFUL. GROOVY. EXPERIMENTAL.

Influenced by music and counterculture, psychedelic typography used distorted letters, flowing shapes, bright colors, and creative layouts to capture attention.

1980s

Neon & Digital

FUTURISTIC. LOUD. ELECTRIC.

The 1980s brought neon colors, futuristic lettering, chrome effects, and digital-inspired designs. Typography became bold and energetic.

1990s

Grunge & Experimental

RAW. UNUSUAL. REBELLIOUS.

The 1990s challenged traditional design rules. Distressed fonts, irregular layouts, pixelated lettering, and unconventional typography became popular.
